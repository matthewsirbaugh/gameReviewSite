There's something profoundly unsettling about *Liminal Realms*, the latest offering from indie studio Echo Collective. It's not the kind of unease that comes from jump scares or grotesque imagery—though the game has its share of both—but rather a deeper, more existential disquiet that lingers long after you've set down the controller. This is a game that understands memory as both archive and prison, and it uses that understanding to craft one of the most emotionally resonant experiences I've encountered this year.

*Liminal Realms* tells the story of Elara, a woman navigating a dreamlike landscape constructed from fragments of her past. The game's central mechanic is memory manipulation: you can rewind, fast-forward, and even alter the events of Elara's life, but each change ripples through the narrative in unexpected ways. It's a concept that's been explored before—*Life is Strange* and *The Stanley Parable* come to mind—but *Liminal Realms* distinguishes itself through its commitment to ambiguity and its refusal to provide easy answers.

From a technical standpoint, the game is remarkably polished for an indie title. The art direction, which blends hand-painted backgrounds with subtle 3D elements, creates a visual language that feels both timeless and contemporary. The soundtrack, composed entirely of ambient soundscapes and minimalist piano pieces, perfectly complements the game's meditative pacing. There are moments when the game simply lets you sit with a memory, watching it unfold without any mechanical intervention, and these quiet interludes are among its strongest moments.

The narrative design is where *Liminal Realms* truly shines. The game doesn't present memory as a linear sequence of events but as a web of interconnected moments, each one capable of being viewed from multiple perspectives. Early in the game, you witness a conversation between Elara and her mother, but as you progress, you unlock new angles on this same moment—the mother's perspective, the perspective of a child watching from the doorway, even the perspective of objects in the room. This multi-perspective approach to storytelling is ambitious, and while it occasionally leads to narrative confusion, it more often results in moments of genuine revelation.

Where the game stumbles slightly is in its pacing. The first two hours are deliberately slow, establishing the game's contemplative tone, but this may test the patience of players expecting more immediate engagement. Additionally, some of the memory manipulation puzzles feel arbitrary, requiring trial-and-error rather than genuine understanding of the game's systems. These are minor quibbles, however, in what is otherwise a masterfully crafted experience.

The game's ending—and I'll avoid spoilers here—is deliberately ambiguous, leaving many questions unanswered. Some players may find this frustrating, but I found it refreshing. *Liminal Realms* isn't interested in providing closure so much as it is in exploring the ways we construct meaning from our past. It's a game that trusts its audience to sit with uncertainty, and that trust is rewarded with one of the most thoughtful meditations on memory and loss in recent gaming history.

**Score: 8/10**

*Liminal Realms* is a game that will likely divide audiences. Those seeking clear answers and traditional narrative structures may find it frustrating. But for players willing to engage with its ambiguity and embrace its contemplative pace, it offers a deeply moving exploration of how we remember, how we forget, and how the past continues to shape us long after it's gone.
