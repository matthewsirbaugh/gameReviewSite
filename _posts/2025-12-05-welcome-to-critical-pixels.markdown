If you're reading this... welcome! 

Since the first time I booted up my Game Boy Color as a child way back in 2002, video games have fascinated me. On a technical level, the idea that you could fit a whole world in stationary metal sparked a curiosity that eventually brought me to my current profession of software development. Likewise, the simple yet endearing narratives I found in my GBC evolved with the medium into art that could stand alongside any book or film in terms of literary excellence. Games like Disco Elysium, The Last of Us, Stanley Parable, Bioshock, etc. 

While there were forums where people could discuss games that often had cogent insights, there were very few outlets that offered academic level analysis of games as literature, which is the framing I've come to love. In fact, to my mind, the only place to find in depth game analysis of that calibre would be through YouTube video essays, of which there are many incredible creators who I owe for inspiring me. Unfortunatley, incredible as they are, they're only human, and they have the caveat of often having inconsistent upload schedules, going months without new videos because of the time required to record gameplay, write a script, record the voiceover, and then edit all of that into an often 2hr+ length video. 

Don't get me wrong, I love listening to them while doing the dishes, watching them while eating meals, everything you'd do with a podcast or audiobook essentially. But I've always wanted that kind of literary analysis in a written form. I think there's a number of advantages to that format. It's faster to consume, it's easier to reference either in the future or when introducing the concepts to colleagues, and ultimately it's just the preferred medium for some users, especially when it comes to longer form academic style content. 

And so, my goal with this website is simple: provide the kind of deep technical and literary analysis I've enjoyed, and offer it in a format that's easily digestable. My first few posts will be a mix of content as I narrow down the style and tone I'd like to set going forward. If you're reading this far into the future, hopefully I've figured things out.
